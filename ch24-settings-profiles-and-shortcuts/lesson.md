# 24. Settings, Profiles, and Keyboard Shortcuts

Visual Studio remembers how you like it. The theme, the fonts, the windows you keep open, and the shortcuts you use all live in settings. Most of the time you never open them. But when you move to a new computer, want to share a setup with a colleague, or find that a shortcut gets in your way, the settings are where you go.

This chapter shows the Options dialog, how to save your settings to a file and bring them back, what a keyboard mapping scheme is, and how to rebind a single command. It uses Visual Studio's default keymap, so the shortcuts in the earlier chapters still apply.

A rebind is saved on your computer. Changing a shortcut does not change it for your teammates, and it does not change it for the solution you have open. Keep that in mind when you write instructions for anyone else.

Settings files can also hold details about your computer, such as folder paths and account names. The second lesson shows how to check a settings file before you share it.
