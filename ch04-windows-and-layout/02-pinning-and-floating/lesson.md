# Pinning, floating, and docking windows

Each tool window, such as the Solution Explorer, the Error List, or the Output window, can sit in one of four states:

- **Docked and pinned:** the window has a fixed place beside the editor and stays open.
- **Auto Hide:** the window slides away to the edge when you click somewhere else, and comes back when you point at its tab.
- **Floating:** the window is a separate window you can move anywhere, including to a second monitor.
- **Tabbed:** the window shares a space with another window, and you switch between them with tabs at the top.

## Pinning and Auto Hide

The pushpin at the top right of a window's title bar switches between pinned and Auto Hide. A pin pointing down keeps the window open. A pin turned sideways means Auto Hide is on, and the window tucks itself away. Click the pin to change it.

Auto Hide is useful on a small screen, where you want the editor to have as much room as possible.

To float a window, press and hold its title bar, then drag it out of the zone where it is docked. Let go once it is away from the editor and the other docked windows. The window becomes separate, and you can drag it by its title bar to any spot on the screen.

## Docking a window again

To put a floating window back, drag it by its title bar toward the docked area of the editor. Visual Studio shows guide arrows as you drag. Move the window over the arrow for the side you want, and release it. The window snaps into place.

> **If a window disappears:** a window with Auto Hide on is still there, but tucked away at an edge. Look for its tab on the edge of the window, or use the reset in the last lesson of this chapter to bring every window back.
