# Checking the publisher and license

Click an extension in the Browse list to open its details page. Figure 22.2 shows a details page, with the publisher's name, a description, and links to more information.

![Figure 22.2 - The details page of an extension](images/ch22-extension-details.png "width=75%")

*Figure 22.2 - The details page of an extension*

Check three things before you install. First, the publisher. Is it a company or person you recognize, and does the listing link to a website you can check? A verified publisher mark, where it appears, is a useful sign, but it is not a guarantee. Second, the license. The details page links to the license terms. Read them, and check that they allow the use you need. Third, what the extension does. Read its description and its permissions, and ask whether it needs access to your code or your sign-in.

Licenses matter for work. A permissive license, such as MIT or Apache 2.0, usually allows commercial use in closed-source projects. A copyleft license, such as GPL, can require you to release your own code under the same terms. If the license is copyleft, or you cannot tell what it allows, stop, and escalate the question to the people who approve software at your organization before you install.

## Try it

1. Open the details page of any extension in the Browse list.
2. Note the publisher, and find the license link.
3. Close the page without installing it.
