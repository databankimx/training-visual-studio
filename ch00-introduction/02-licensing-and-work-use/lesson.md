# Licensing and work use

Visual Studio Community is free, and it is a very good IDE. It is also licensed for specific uses, and the license is the first thing to check before you install anything. Read this lesson before you go on, because the rest of the course assumes you have.

> The TLDR is that Visual Studio Community is free for learning, but at DataBank it cannot be used for work. A more detailed explanation follows.

## What Microsoft allows

Microsoft's license for Visual Studio Community depends on who you are and what you are doing with it. The rules, as they appear on Microsoft's Visual Studio pricing page, come down to four points.

- **Individual developers.** Any individual can use Visual Studio Community to create and test their own applications, whether those applications are free or paid.
- **Classroom learning, academic research, and open source.** Within any organization, an unlimited number of users can use Community for classroom learning, academic research, and contributing to or developing open-source projects.
- **Non-enterprise organizations.** An organization that is not an enterprise can let up to five users use Community for other development work.
- **Enterprise organizations.** An organization is an enterprise if it has more than 250 PCs or more than $1 million US dollars in annual revenue. An enterprise may use Community only for open-source work, academic research, and classroom learning. Any other use, including ordinary commercial development, needs a paid edition.

The rule that matters for most people is the last one. Notice that the word "enterprise" is not about how big your team is. It is about the size of the organization, measured by PCs or revenue, so a small team inside a large company can still fall under it.

## What that means for DataBank

DataBank is an enterprise organization. It has more than 250 PCs and more than $1 million in annual revenue, so the enterprise rule applies to all of it.

That leaves Visual Studio Community with one permitted use in this course: learning. It is approved for this tutorial, and for the classroom and training material it supports.

It is not approved for DataBank work. That includes building or testing any code for a DataBank project, and it includes anything built with Community that is delivered to a DataBank customer. If your role needs Visual Studio at work, your manager must request a paid license for you, Professional or Enterprise, through the usual channel. Do not install Community on a work machine and start a project on it "just to try something" for a customer.

> **The line to remember:** Community is for learning here. Work at DataBank uses a licensed edition that your manager has requested for you.

If anything here seems to conflict with those terms, the terms win, and you should tell your manager.
